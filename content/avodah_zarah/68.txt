Avodah Zarah 68 – Mouse That Fell Into Beer

It stayed there, soaking for more than 24 hours, and was therefore considered cooked, imparting flavor to the beer. Rav ruled the beer forbidden.<br />
<br />
The student related this to Rav Sheshet and told him that Rav must consider the forbidden flavor that ruins the taste as  forbidden. "Not necessarily," Rav Sheshet told them. In general, Rav may consider such  food permitted. Only here, since people are generally disgusted by mice, the Torah, by prohibiting mice, told us to avoid even their flavor. The students told him that they don't see this logic in such cases as semen, and furthermore, Rav Shimi said that in some places they go on the tables of kings.<br />
<br />
Therefore, Rava concluded that when a forbidden flavor ruins the taste, the dish remains kosher. Here, however, he was not sure why Rav prohibited the beer. Rav might have held that bad flavor made it non-kosher, and then the law would not agree with Rav. It could also be that Rav thought the mouse's flavor improved the beer, and in that case, the law prohibiting flavor would agree with him.<br />
